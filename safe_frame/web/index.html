Safe Frame

ClickHouse track · version-lineage safety

The master passed. The version people watched was never checked.

One film becomes dozens of cuts, grades, frame rates and ad-break versions. Safe Frame searches a whole distribution catalogue for a photosensitivity risk that a conversion introduced — even when the approved master was clean.

Transition measurements are aligned on presentation time in ClickHouse. Both published flash rules are evaluated across every rendition in a single pass through the official mcp-clickhouse server, and master/rendition isolation happens inside the same query. Gemini explains the rows; SQL decides.

Who this is for: the studio crew that ships the versions — distribution QC and encoding teams who sign off a master and then send dozens of derived assets out the door.

— transition measurements are loaded and waiting to be searched.

Why this matters

A rendition is a new chance to hurt somebody.

Photosensitive epilepsy is rare, documented, and regulated. The standards are public and the analysers are mature — but they were built to judge a file, not to judge a file against the one that was already approved.

~1 in 4,000

people in the general population have photosensitive epilepsy. It is roughly twice as common in women, and most often begins before age 20.

Epilepsy Foundation review, Fisher et al., Epilepsia 2022
685 children

were taken to hospital across Japan after a single television broadcast on 16 December 1997. Two stayed in hospital more than two weeks.

The 1997 broadcast incident
Rule 2.12

of the Ofcom Broadcasting Code requires broadcasters to keep risk to photosensitive viewers low. WCAG 2.3.1 sets equivalent thresholds for anything delivered on the web.

Ofcom §2 · W3C WCAG 2.3.1
Standard practice puts the quality gate at ingest, before transcode, because a bad master multiplies across the whole ladder. That gate is doing its job. It simply is not looking at what the transcode adds after the master was signed off.

What already exists

Detection, repair, viewer-side dimming and networked batch analysis are all mature. Harding-style analysers test luminance flashes, red flashes and spatial patterns, and fail a file that breaches them. We claim none of this as ours.

What we could not find

A documented check that compares a rendition against its own approved parent and attributes a newly-introduced violation to the transform that caused it. Our search covered public product documentation; it cannot prove no private integration exists.

Does it actually work

      
Scored against a ground truth it cannot see.

      
The fair objection to a synthetic demonstration is that it is circular — data was generated with flashes in it, and then the flashes were found. So the planted set is recovered from the generator’s own random decisions, without reading a single measurement, and compared against what the criteria returned.

      
sql/008_ground_truth.sql recomputes the decisions that chose where to plant. It never touches luma_delta, red_delta, luma_min, changed_area_fraction or direction. Two queries, produced by independent means; agreement between them is a result rather than a restatement.

      
The catalogue holds two kinds of row, and they are scored separately on purpose. Most are authored — SQL decided what each measurement should be. The measured_* titles are not: they are constructed RGB frames pushed through the same measurement code a decoder would feed, so every value is the output of relative_luminance and red_flash_mask over real pixel arrays. If the detector only scored well on numbers we wrote, the corpus would be doing the work.

Why the decoys are the point

          
Recall alone can be bought by flagging everything. The corpus carries renditions that carry a real burst and are still not regressions — because their approved master has the same burst at the same presentation time — and a cohort that clears every floor but sits above the published darker-image ceiling. Precision is measured against those.

        

        

          
What this does not show

          
Agreement with a synthetic ground truth we authored. It demonstrates that the implementation does what the criteria say at catalogue scale, and it is not evidence about real footage, not a comparison against a certified analyser, and not a clinical result.

Dozens of findings, or a handful of broken profiles?

    Criteria provenance
    
      
    Every threshold traces to a published definition.
    
      
    Nothing here is a number we chose. Each one is the published criterion, and the one rule we have not implemented is named rather than implied.
    
      
    
        
    
          
    
            
    What is testedThreshold in Safe FramePublished definition
    
            
              
    
                General flash — amplitude
                luma_delta ≥ 0.10
                “a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum relative luminance…” — WCAG 2.3.1
              
    
              
    
                General flash — darker image
                luma_min < 0.80
                “…where the relative luminance of the darker image is below 0.80”. A swing between two bright states is not a general flash, however large. We were missing this until we audited against the published text — it was producing false positives. — WCAG 2.3.1
              
    
              
    
                Red flash
                per pixel: R/(R+G+B) ≥ 0.8
    and |Δ(R−G−B)×320| > 20
    then red_delta ≥ 0.20
    no luminance condition    		
                “any pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red”, where “for either or both states… R/(R+G+B) >= 0.8, and the change in the value of (R-G-B)x320 is > 20”. People are more sensitive to red flashing, so it gets its own test — deliberately with no luminance condition. The pixel test runs in safe_frame/metrics.py and yields red_delta, the fraction of the frame that satisfied it; the SQL thresholds that column at 0.20. That floor is lower than the 0.25 area condition, so the red rule is deliberately the more sensitive of the two. — WCAG 2.3.1
              
    
              
    
                Relative luminance
                0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B
    over linearised sRGB    		
                “if RsRGB <= 0.04045 then R = RsRGB/12.92 else R = ((RsRGB+0.055)/1.055) ^ 2.4”. We were weighting the gamma-encoded values without linearising them. — W3C
              
    
              
    
                Affected area
                changed_area_fraction ≥ 0.25
    approximated    		
                WCAG asks for “25% of any 10 degree visual field” — an angular measure needing screen size and viewing distance a file does not carry. We use the fraction of the frame, which matches the Ofcom/ITU wording (“a quarter of the screen area”) and, per a 2024 gap analysis, every guideline except WCAG. Against WCAG specifically it can under-report. — WCAG 2.3.1 · Ofcom §2
              
    
              
    
                Rate
                > 6 opposing transitions
    in any 1000 ms    		
                “more than three flashes in any one second period”. One flash is a pair of opposing transitions, so three flashes is six transitions — WCAG 2.3.1
              
    
              
    
                Spatial pattern
                not implemented
                Harding-style analysers also test regular spatial patterns. Safe Frame does not, and will not flag a striped or checkerboard pattern that breaches the spatial criteria.
              
    
                
          
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    What auditing against the source found
    
          
    We could not obtain review by a qualified photosensitive-epilepsy professional, so we audited the implementation against the published texts instead and wrote down what was wrong. Three defects, all of which changed results: relative luminance was never linearised; the darker-image condition was missing entirely; and saturated red was a plausible-looking proxy rather than the published test. The guidelines are themselves not harmonised, so passing here is not compliance with every regime.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    What that was worth
    
          
    On this catalogue the missing darker-image condition alone produced 13 false positives — renditions reported as general flashes where both states were above the 0.80 ceiling the published test sets. The sweep returns 44 with the condition and 57 without it. The corpus now carries a bright-on-bright control cohort so the condition cannot quietly go missing again.
    
        
    
      
    
      
    This audit is not equivalent to expert review, and it does not establish efficacy. It is written so it can be checked: every threshold above quotes the source. Safe Frame is an open pre-check implementing published criteria. It is not certified, not broadcaster-approved, and not a medical device. It does not replace certified delivery testing.

    Decision boundary
    
            
    Arithmetic owns the verdict.
    
            
    The AI cannot declare a video safe. It explains rows the database returned, and asks a human QC operator to act.
    
            
    If the MCP query fails, or its rows cannot be parsed, the endpoint returns 502. It never substitutes Gemini or a local guess in the live path.
    
            
    tests/test_sql_parity.py runs the reference Python detector and this SQL over identical randomised inputs, for both rules, and requires exact agreement — so the catalogue sweep cannot silently drift from the per-file pre-check.
    
            
    Each rule is windowed over its own qualifying transitions, so one rule can never pad the other’s count, and the isolation step is keyed on rule: a master that already flashed in luminance does not excuse a rendition that introduced a red flash.
    
          
    
          
    
-- each rule gets its own qualifying set
    
WHERE luma_delta >= 0.10
    
  AND luma_min < 0.80 -- the darker image
    
  AND changed_area_fraction >= 0.25
    
  AND direction != 'flat'

    
WHERE red_delta >= 0.20 -- no luma floor
    
  AND changed_area_fraction >= 0.25
    
  AND direction != 'flat'

    
WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY asset_id
    
  ORDER BY pts_ms
    
  RANGE BETWEEN CURRENT ROW AND 999 FOLLOWING)

    
WHERE win_transitions > 6
    
  AND win_dir_min != win_dir_max
