Safe Frame

ClickHouse track · version-lineage safety

The master passed. The version people watched was never checked.

One film becomes dozens of cuts, grades, frame rates and ad-break versions. Safe Frame searches a whole distribution catalogue for a photosensitivity risk that a conversion introduced — even when the approved master was clean.

Transition measurements are aligned on presentation time in ClickHouse. Both published flash rules are evaluated across every rendition in a single pass through the official mcp-clickhouse server, and master/rendition isolation happens inside the same query. Gemini explains the rows; SQL decides.

Why this matters

A rendition is a new chance to hurt somebody.

Photosensitive epilepsy is rare, documented, and regulated. The standards are public and the analysers are mature — but they were built to judge a file, not to judge a file against the one that was already approved.

~1 in 4,000

people in the general population have photosensitive epilepsy. It is roughly twice as common in women, and most often begins before age 20.

Epilepsy Foundation review, Fisher et al., Epilepsia 2022
685 children

were taken to hospital across Japan after a single television broadcast on 16 December 1997. Two stayed in hospital more than two weeks.

The 1997 broadcast incident
Rule 2.12

of the Ofcom Broadcasting Code requires broadcasters to keep risk to photosensitive viewers low. WCAG 2.3.1 sets equivalent thresholds for anything delivered on the web.

Ofcom §2 · W3C WCAG 2.3.1
Standard practice puts the quality gate at ingest, before transcode, because a bad master multiplies across the whole ladder. That gate is doing its job. It simply is not looking at what the transcode adds after the master was signed off.

What already exists

Detection, repair, viewer-side dimming and networked batch analysis are all mature. Harding-style analysers test luminance flashes, red flashes and spatial patterns, and fail a file that breaches them. We claim none of this as ours.

What we could not find

A documented check that compares a rendition against its own approved parent and attributes a newly-introduced violation to the transform that caused it. Our search covered public product documentation; it cannot prove no private integration exists.

Criteria provenance

      
Every threshold traces to a published definition.

      
Nothing here is a number we chose. Each one is the published criterion, and the one rule we have not implemented is named rather than implied.

      

        

          

            
What is testedThreshold in Safe FramePublished definition

            
              

                General flash
                luma_delta ≥ 0.10
                “a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of 10% or more of the maximum relative luminance” — WCAG 2.3.1
              

              

                Red flash
                red_delta ≥ 0.20
no luminance floor		
                “any pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated red”. People are more sensitive to red flashing than other colours, so it gets its own test — and deliberately no luminance condition — WCAG 2.3.1
              

              

                Affected area
                changed_area_fraction ≥ 0.25
                flashes occupying more than “25% of any 10 degree visual field on the screen” at typical viewing distance — WCAG 2.3.1
              

              

                Rate
                > 6 opposing transitions
in any 1000 ms		
                “more than three flashes in any one second period”. One flash is a pair of opposing transitions, so three flashes is six transitions — WCAG 2.3.1
              

              

                Spatial pattern
                not implemented
                Harding-style analysers also test regular spatial patterns. Safe Frame does not, and will not flag a striped or checkerboard pattern that breaches the spatial criteria.

Decision boundary

            
Arithmetic owns the verdict.

            
The AI cannot declare a video safe. It explains rows the database returned, and asks a human QC operator to act.

            
If the MCP query fails, or its rows cannot be parsed, the endpoint returns 502. It never substitutes Gemini or a local guess in the live path.

            
tests/test_sql_parity.py runs the reference Python detector and this SQL over identical randomised inputs, for both rules, and requires exact agreement — so the catalogue sweep cannot silently drift from the per-file pre-check.

            
Each rule is windowed over its own qualifying transitions, so one rule can never pad the other’s count, and the isolation step is keyed on rule: a master that already flashed in luminance does not excuse a rendition that introduced a red flash.

          

          

-- each rule gets its own qualifying set

WHERE luma_delta >= 0.10

  AND changed_area_fraction >= 0.25

  AND direction != 'flat'


WHERE red_delta >= 0.20 -- no luma floor

  AND changed_area_fraction >= 0.25

  AND direction != 'flat'


WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY asset_id

  ORDER BY pts_ms

  RANGE BETWEEN CURRENT ROW AND 999 FOLLOWING)


WHERE win_transitions > 6

  AND win_dir_min != win_dir_max


decision_source = clickhouse_sql_via_official_mcp
